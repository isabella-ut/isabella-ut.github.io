I'm a doctoral researcher at WelGain, an initiative funded by the Research Council of Norway examining the "visibility bias" in welfare policy evaluation — the tendency for fiscal costs to dominate public and political debate while social gains remain unmeasured.

I'm a PhD candidate at the Faculty of Social Sciences, Norwegian University of Science and Technology (NTNU), where I work on WelGain. I hold an MSc in Data Science for Public Policy from the Hertie School (Germany) and a BSc in Economics from Pontificia Universidad Javeriana (Colombia).

Before joining NTNU, I spent over five years in applied research and policy consultancy. Most recently, I worked as a pre-doctoral researcher at the International Security and Development Center (ISDC), applying causal inference and machine learning methods to evaluate welfare and livelihood interventions in humanitarian contexts across the Middle East, Africa, and Latin America. Earlier, I worked as a junior researcher at CoreWoman, contributing to projects on gender-lens investing, the care economy, financial inclusion, and women's economic empowerment across the Global South; as a data visualization consultant on a project with the Alliance of Bioversity International and CIAT; and as a research and data assistant at the Applied Economics Lab (LEA) of Pontificia Universidad Javeriana.

My research interests lie at the intersection of applied microeconomics, causal inference, and machine learning, with a particular focus on evaluating the effectiveness of welfare and social protection policies.
