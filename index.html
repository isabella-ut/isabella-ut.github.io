I study why the fiscal costs of welfare reforms dominate public debate while their social gains stay unmeasured.

I'm a PhD candidate at the Department of Social Work and Health Science at the Norwegian University of Science and Technology (NTNU), where I work on WelGain, a project funded by the Research Council of Norway, supervised by Prof. Roberto Iacono. My research is interdisciplinary, working at the intersection of applied microeconomics, causal inference, and public policy, with a focus on welfare, inequality, and social protection.

Before starting my PhD, I was a junior researcher at the International Security and Development Center (ISDC) in Berlin, where I designed and implemented experimental research and impact evaluations of livelihood, gender, and agricultural interventions in Syria, Türkiye, and Sudan. I hold an MSc in Data Science for Public Policy from the Hertie School and a BSc in Economics from Pontificia Universidad Javeriana, Cali. I'm committed to rigorous, policy-relevant research that bridges economics, statistics, and machine learning.
