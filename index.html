I study the economic sustainability of welfare reforms — and why fiscal costs so often dominate public debate while social gains stay unmeasured.

I am an incoming PhD student at the Norwegian University of Science and Technology (NTNU), where I work on the WelGain project, funded by the Research Council of Norway, under the supervision of Prof. Roberto Iacono. My research sits at the intersection of applied microeconomics, causal inference, and public policy, with a focus on welfare, inequality, and social protection.

Before starting my PhD, I worked as a junior researcher at the International Security and Development Center (ISDC) in Berlin, running impact evaluations of livelihood, gender, and agricultural interventions across Syria, Türkiye, and Sudan. I hold an MSc in Data Science for Public Policy from the Hertie School and a BSc in Economics from Pontificia Universidad Javeriana, Cali.

Research

Visibility bias in welfare policy evaluation — the WelGain project 2026 – 2029
NTNU · Research Council of Norway · Supervisor: Prof. Roberto Iacono

My doctoral work examines the tendency for the fiscal costs of welfare reforms to dominate public and political debate while their social gains remain unmeasured. The project develops a cost–benefit framework that integrates intangible welfare gains, applies it to two Norwegian reforms using counterfactual causal inference methods, and tests experimentally whether providing citizens with evidence on hidden gains changes support for social programs.
